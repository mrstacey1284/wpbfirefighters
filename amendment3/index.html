Fire Assessment Fee

A fire fee cannot backfill $31M+

The Fire Assessment Fee is a separate charge that helps fund part of the city's fire protection services. However, it can not be used to fund EMS and does not completely fund fire protection services.

If Amendment 3 cuts $31M+ from city property taxes, pressure will grow to raise other local revenues, including the Fire Assessment Fee, to try to backfill fire costs. That still leaves EMS and police exposed on the General Fund side. Raising a flat or parcel-based fire fee to compensate hits lower-income areas the hardest. Those households get less benefit from a bigger homestead exemption and feel fee increases more.

Tax relief that shifts the bill onto fees is not real relief for the households already stretched thin.

FAQ

Direct answers

Does Amendment 3 eliminate West Palm Beach Fire?

No. It does not close West Palm Beach Fire or require service be provided by another entity. It reduces the city’s property-tax revenue, the same revenue that pays for fire, EMS, and police.

That loss could lead to reduced staffing and less resources availble to answer 911 calls. With no plan or dedicated revenue replacement written in the amendement, West Palm Beach Firefigthers are left trying to accomplish more with less.

Does it guarantee today’s staffing or response capacity?

No. Amendment 3 does not protect today’s staffing, units, or the ability to keep answering calls the way the city does now.

It shrinks city property-tax revenue without a plan to replace that money. Fewer dollars in the General Fund put pressure on the people who respond when residents call.

Is there a dedicated replacement formula for Fire Rescue or EMS?

No. The measure does not include a dedicated replacement formula for West Palm Beach Fire Department or EMS.

The $31M+ figure is lost city property-tax revenue. Nothing on the ballot assigns a recurring revenue source to put that money back into fire or EMS.

Do firefighters oppose all tax relief?

No. Firefighters support tax relief for all West Palm Beach homeowners. Amendment 3 is right idea with poor implementation that will hurts public safety.

A No vote clears the path for real, logical tax reform that can be implemented while protecting public safety. Support tax relief that does not gut the funding base for West Palm Beach fire, EMS, and police.

Is schools funding safe under amendment 3?

Yes. Amendment 3’s big new homestead exemption applies to non-school taxes. School taxes keep the narrower exemption structure.

City millage that funds fire, EMS, and police does not get that carve-out. Schools are protected. Public safety is not.

Can the Fire Assessment Fee backfill the $31M+ hit?

No. Today’s Fire Assessment brings in about $8.7M. It is a separate charge that helps fund fire services. It does not fully fund fire and can not be used to fund EMS at all. It simply cannot backfill a $31M+ ad valorem hit.

If pressure grows to raise that fee to chase the gap, a flat or parcel-based increase hits lower-income areas the hardest. Those households get less benefit from a bigger homestead exemption and feel fee increases more. Tax relief that shifts the bill onto fees is not real relief for the households already stretched thin.

What is the impact to West Palm Beach's bottomline?

Under the 2028 ($250,000 exemption) assumptions, the City of West Palm Beach stands to lose $31M+ a year in property-tax revenue. That is about 21% of the city’s roughly $149M in FY26 property-tax revenue.

Property taxes make up about 52.6% of the city’s General Fund, the same fund that pays for West Palm Beach Fire Rescue, EMS, and Police. The revenue-loss figure comes from Palm Beach County Property Appraiser figures released in June 2026 and reported in the Palm Beach Post city-by-city table. The FY26 context is the City of West Palm Beach FY26 Budget in Brief.

What does Amendment 3 change, and when?

If voters approve Amendment 3 on the November 2026 ballot, the changes take effect Jan. 1, 2027. The non-school homestead exemption moves to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.

The cap on non-homestead assessment growth falls from 10% to 5%. Those changes hit non-school taxes. They do not come with a replacement for city fire, EMS, and police funding.
